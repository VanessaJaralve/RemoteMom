# RemoteMom Interview Script And Validation Scorecard

Use this while RemoteMom is still validating the niche. The goal is to learn whether remote working moms have a painful, repeated coordination problem and whether RemoteMom is pointed at the right paid feature.

## RemoteMom Interview Script

Target length: 15 minutes.

Ideal respondent: remote or hybrid working mom, especially with two or more children.

Tone: curious, casual, and specific. Do not pitch first. Let them describe their real day before showing the solution.

### Opening

Thanks for taking a few minutes. I am validating a personal app idea called RemoteMom. I am not trying to sell you anything today. I am trying to understand how remote working moms actually manage work, kids, groceries, schedules, and health reminders.

### Ask These Questions In Order

1. Can you walk me through a normal weekday from morning to bedtime?
2. Where does the day usually start to feel scattered or hard to manage?
3. What are you using today to keep track of tasks, groceries, kid schedules, and reminders?
4. What falls through the cracks most often?
5. When something is missed, what is the consequence?
6. Who else helps manage the family schedule or errands?
7. If another adult helps, how do you share updates today?
8. You selected a premium feature in the form. What made that one stand out?
9. What would make RemoteMom useful enough to open every day?
10. What would make you stop using it after a week?
11. If RemoteMom saved you time or stress, what price would feel fair?
12. Can I follow up later with a prototype or a few screenshots?

### Listen For

- Repeated pain, not one-time inconvenience
- Multi-child coordination needs
- Partner, caregiver, or family sharing needs
- Existing workaround complexity
- Emotional language such as overwhelmed, scattered, anxious, forgot, guilty, or too many apps
- Willingness to try a prototype or pay

## Validation Scorecard

Fill one row per survey response or interview.

| Field | What To Record |
| --- | --- |
| Date | Date of response or interview |
| Source | Facebook, Instagram, direct message, friend referral |
| Mom Profile | Remote, hybrid, number of children, ages if shared |
| Hardest Area | Child schedule, groceries, health, work, all |
| Premium Feature | Sharing, multiple children, reminders, calendar sync, cloud backup |
| Price Comfort | Free only, monthly price, annual price |
| Interview Permission | Yes, maybe, no |
| Pain Strength | 1 to 5 |
| Frequency | Daily, weekly, occasional |
| Current Workaround | Notes app, calendar, partner text, paper, memory, multiple apps |
| Sharing Need | None, partner, caregiver, family, school helper |
| Willingness To Pay | 1 to 5 |
| Prototype Interest | Yes, maybe, no |
| Key Quote | One sentence in their own words |
| Follow-up Action | Thank, ask follow-up, invite to call, send prototype |

### Score Definitions

Pain strength:

- 1 = Mild annoyance
- 2 = Noticeable but manageable
- 3 = Recurring problem with real friction
- 4 = Stressful problem that affects the day
- 5 = Strong pain with missed tasks, conflict, cost, or emotional burden

Willingness to pay:

- 1 = Free only
- 2 = Might pay later
- 3 = Comfortable with a low annual plan
- 4 = Comfortable with monthly or annual premium if useful
- 5 = Actively asks for paid access, sharing, or early prototype

## Decision Thresholds

Keep collecting:

- Fewer than 10 total responses
- Fewer than 3 interviews or follow-up conversations
- No repeated pain pattern yet

Strong early signal:

- 10 or more responses
- 5 or more people with pain strength 4 or 5
- 3 or more people willing to interview
- One premium feature appears in at least 40 percent of responses
- At least 30 percent choose a paid price option

Build next:

- The same pain appears repeatedly in surveys and interviews
- People describe current workarounds without prompting
- People ask when they can try it
- Paid feature interest clusters around one clear feature, such as sharing or multiple children

Do not build yet:

- People say the idea is nice but cannot name a recent moment when they needed it
- Most respondents choose free only
- Premium feature interest is scattered with no clear pattern
- The strongest signal comes only from friends being supportive

## Weekly Review

Review the scorecard once per week and answer:

1. What pain showed up most often?
2. What premium feature showed up most often?
3. How many people showed willingness to pay?
4. How many people agreed to talk or see a prototype?
5. What should be changed in the landing page message?
6. What should not be built yet?
